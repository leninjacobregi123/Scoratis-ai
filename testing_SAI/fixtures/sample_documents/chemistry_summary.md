# Chemistry Summary: Atomic Structure and Chemical Bonding

## Atomic Structure

### Subatomic Particles

| Particle | Charge | Mass (amu) | Location |
|----------|--------|------------|----------|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Electron | -1 | ~0 | Electron cloud |

### Atomic Number and Mass Number
- **Atomic number (Z):** Number of protons (defines the element)
- **Mass number (A):** Protons + Neutrons
- **Isotopes:** Same element, different number of neutrons

### Electron Configuration
Electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy (Aufbau principle).

Order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p...

**Examples:**
- Carbon (6): 1s2 2s2 2p2
- Oxygen (8): 1s2 2s2 2p4
- Iron (26): 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 4s2 3d6

### Valence Electrons
- Electrons in the outermost shell
- Determine chemical properties and bonding behavior
- Group number often indicates valence electrons (for main groups)

## Chemical Bonding

### Ionic Bonds
- Transfer of electrons between atoms
- Forms between metals and nonmetals
- Results in electrostatic attraction between ions

**Example:** NaCl (sodium chloride)
- Na loses 1 electron -> Na+
- Cl gains 1 electron -> Cl-
- Ionic crystal lattice forms

**Properties of ionic compounds:**
- High melting/boiling points
- Conduct electricity when dissolved or melted
- Brittle crystalline solids

### Covalent Bonds
- Sharing of electrons between atoms
- Forms between nonmetals
- Can be single, double, or triple bonds

**Types:**
1. **Nonpolar covalent:** Equal sharing (same electronegativity)
2. **Polar covalent:** Unequal sharing (different electronegativity)

**Examples:**
- H2: Single bond, nonpolar
- O2: Double bond
- N2: Triple bond
- H2O: Polar covalent

### Metallic Bonds
- "Sea of electrons" model
- Delocalized electrons shared among metal atoms
- Explains conductivity, malleability, ductility

## Molecular Geometry (VSEPR Theory)

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion theory predicts molecular shapes.

| Electron Pairs | Molecular Geometry | Example |
|----------------|-------------------|---------|
| 2 | Linear | CO2 |
| 3 | Trigonal planar | BF3 |
| 4 | Tetrahedral | CH4 |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal | PCl5 |
| 6 | Octahedral | SF6 |

**Effect of lone pairs:**
- Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs
- H2O: 4 electron pairs, but bent (2 lone pairs)
- NH3: 4 electron pairs, but trigonal pyramidal (1 lone pair)

## Electronegativity

**Electronegativity:** Ability of an atom to attract electrons in a bond.

**Trends:**
- Increases across a period (left to right)
- Decreases down a group
- Fluorine is most electronegative (4.0)

**Bond polarity:**
- Difference < 0.5: Nonpolar covalent
- Difference 0.5-1.7: Polar covalent
- Difference > 1.7: Ionic

## Intermolecular Forces

### Types (weakest to strongest):
1. **London dispersion forces:** Present in all molecules
2. **Dipole-dipole forces:** Between polar molecules
3. **Hydrogen bonding:** Special case of dipole-dipole (H bonded to F, O, or N)

### Effects on Properties:
- Stronger intermolecular forces = higher boiling point
- Water has unusually high boiling point due to hydrogen bonding
