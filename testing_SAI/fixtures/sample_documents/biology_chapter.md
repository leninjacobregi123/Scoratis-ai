# Biology Chapter 5: Cellular Respiration

## Overview

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to produce ATP
(adenosine triphosphate), the energy currency of cells.

**Overall Equation:**
C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP (energy)

## Stages of Cellular Respiration

### 1. Glycolysis

**Location:** Cytoplasm
**Input:** 1 glucose molecule (6 carbons)
**Output:** 2 pyruvate molecules (3 carbons each)

Key points:
- Does NOT require oxygen (anaerobic)
- Net gain of 2 ATP
- Produces 2 NADH (electron carriers)
- 10-step process with enzyme catalysis

Steps summary:
1. Energy investment phase (uses 2 ATP)
2. Cleavage phase (splits 6-carbon into two 3-carbons)
3. Energy payoff phase (produces 4 ATP + 2 NADH)

### 2. Pyruvate Oxidation

**Location:** Mitochondrial matrix
**Input:** 2 pyruvate molecules
**Output:** 2 Acetyl-CoA molecules

- Each pyruvate loses CO2 (decarboxylation)
- NAD+ is reduced to NADH
- Coenzyme A attaches to form Acetyl-CoA

### 3. Citric Acid Cycle (Krebs Cycle)

**Location:** Mitochondrial matrix
**Input:** 2 Acetyl-CoA
**Output per cycle:**
- 2 CO2
- 3 NADH
- 1 FADH2
- 1 ATP (or GTP)

The cycle:
1. Acetyl-CoA combines with oxaloacetate to form citrate
2. Series of oxidation reactions release CO2
3. Regenerates oxaloacetate for next cycle

### 4. Electron Transport Chain (ETC)

**Location:** Inner mitochondrial membrane
**Input:** NADH and FADH2 from previous stages
**Output:** Large amounts of ATP (26-28)

Process:
1. Electrons passed through protein complexes (I, II, III, IV)
2. Energy used to pump H+ ions into intermembrane space
3. H+ gradient drives ATP synthase
4. O2 is final electron acceptor, forms H2O

## ATP Yield Summary

| Stage | ATP Produced |
|-------|--------------|
| Glycolysis | 2 ATP |
| Citric Acid Cycle | 2 ATP |
| Electron Transport Chain | 26-28 ATP |
| **Total** | **30-32 ATP** |

## Fermentation (Anaerobic Respiration)

When oxygen is not available, cells use fermentation to regenerate NAD+.

### Lactic Acid Fermentation
- Occurs in muscle cells during intense exercise
- Pyruvate -> Lactate
- Causes muscle fatigue and soreness

### Alcoholic Fermentation
- Occurs in yeast and some bacteria
- Pyruvate -> Ethanol + CO2
- Used in brewing and baking

## Regulation of Cellular Respiration

**Feedback inhibition:**
- High ATP levels inhibit phosphofructokinase (glycolysis)
- High citrate inhibits phosphofructokinase
- Low ATP/high ADP stimulates respiration

## Connection to Photosynthesis

Cellular respiration and photosynthesis are complementary:
- Photosynthesis: CO2 + H2O + light -> Glucose + O2
- Respiration: Glucose + O2 -> CO2 + H2O + ATP

Plants do both processes; animals only do respiration.
