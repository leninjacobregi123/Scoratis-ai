# Physics Notes - Classical Mechanics

## Newton's Laws of Motion

### First Law (Law of Inertia)
An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same
speed and direction unless acted upon by an unbalanced force.

Key concepts:
- Inertia is the resistance to change in motion
- Mass is a measure of inertia
- Examples: seatbelts in cars, objects sliding on ice

### Second Law (F = ma)
The acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass.
Formula: F = m * a

Where:
- F is force (measured in Newtons, N)
- m is mass (measured in kilograms, kg)
- a is acceleration (measured in m/s^2)

Example: A 10 kg object with 20 N force has acceleration of 2 m/s^2

### Third Law (Action-Reaction)
For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Examples:
- Rocket propulsion: gases push down, rocket goes up
- Walking: foot pushes ground backward, ground pushes you forward
- Swimming: hands push water backward, water pushes swimmer forward

## Energy and Work

### Kinetic Energy
KE = (1/2) * m * v^2

The energy possessed by an object due to its motion.

### Potential Energy
PE = m * g * h

The energy possessed by an object due to its position in a gravitational field.

### Conservation of Energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.
Total mechanical energy = KE + PE = constant (in absence of friction)

## Momentum

### Linear Momentum
p = m * v

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity. It is a vector quantity.

### Conservation of Momentum
In a closed system, total momentum before collision equals total momentum after collision.
m1*v1 + m2*v2 = m1*v1' + m2*v2'

### Types of Collisions
1. Elastic: Both momentum and kinetic energy conserved
2. Inelastic: Only momentum conserved, KE lost to heat/deformation
3. Perfectly inelastic: Objects stick together after collision

## Gravity

### Universal Gravitation
F = G * (m1 * m2) / r^2

Where:
- G is gravitational constant (6.67 x 10^-11 N*m^2/kg^2)
- m1, m2 are masses
- r is distance between centers

### Free Fall
- Acceleration due to gravity: g = 9.8 m/s^2 (on Earth)
- All objects fall at same rate (ignoring air resistance)
- Terminal velocity occurs when drag equals weight
